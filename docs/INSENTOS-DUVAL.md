## insentos's 100x template

He opens by saying this is not a trading style. You trade 2x, 3x, 0.5x daily and keep this template in your back pocket for the rare coin that qualifies.

1. **Attention is the only variable.** Memecoins have no intrinsic value. Celebrity, utility, narrative, or funny meme all funnel into attention. A 100x means front-running attention: will people eventually find this worth buying?
2. **Target range.** He excludes the 10k to 1M runs as gambling you cannot size. The template is for coins at $500k to $1M that go to $50M to $100M, where you can size in without worrying about vamps or new-pair trenching.
3. **Criterion 1: it must be new.** 90% of launches are things that were done 100 times. The breakout coins break market ceilings because the thing has never happened before. AMC: a CEO addressing a memecoin. SI: a president renaming a term.
4. **Criterion 2: who is buying.** Check the FOMO leaderboard buyers. UniPC has 800k FOMO followers, so his buy puts 1.6 million eyes on a coin. Names he lists: UniPC, Freddy Gods, Poor Goat. Try to front-run these wallets, not buy on top of them. Smart wallets equal attention.
5. **Criterion 3: list the future catalysts.** Before buying, write out what events could bring attention. For AMC he wrote "Vlad will respond" and was right. If you cannot name a catalyst for a cute dog nobody is posting, there is nothing to hold for.
6. **Exit rule: sell at max virality, sell the news.** The attention curve is the price curve. When the thing you were front-running actually happens (name changed, CEOs stop arguing), that is the top. His quote: "when the attention slows down, the coin goes down." His own AMC error was holding past the peak.
7. **Be online all day.** 10 minutes to an hour a day means you statistically miss it. "You can never miss a lucky day if you're online every day."
8. **Understand virality.** TikTok brain is a real edge. Knowing what people find funny is the skill.
9. **Never trade celebrity tokens.** Nine in ten are scams, even though celebrities bring attention.

## Jack Duval's template

1. **Study trending before you trade.** For every coin on FOMO trending (formerly DexScreener): what is it, why did it run, who bought, why does that buyer matter, what is the narrative. Do this with no trades on. The goal is to anticipate runners earlier each time.
2. **Everything is a derivative.** Nearly every new pair derives from a past giga-runner: ELM from LLM ($250M, AI meta), King of the Hill from Capsy Coin, Giraffe with Cap from WIF ($5B). Knowing the original gives you the ceiling for the derivative. Without that history you cannot gauge ceilings and should not take the trade.
3. **Buy consensus runners.** Relevant meta, relevant trend, lots of volume, real buyers, real traders. Get in relatively early but you do not need to be first. This is what he tells new traders to do, not trenching.
4. **Avoid dev-based and tech coins.** Dev sells, instant zero, you will not get out. 99% of tech coins are redeployed code or rugs. Narrative plays have PvP and FUD but bounded downside.
5. **Map the catalysts, then stick to your guns.** For conviction trades he mentally writes out what should happen and holds through it. He does not aggressively take profit along the way. He admits this has hurt him (AMC) but expects it to pay in a bigger market.
6. **Size down everything the moment you feel resistance.** When PUMP and the Robinhood ecosystem dipped, he cut everything except AMC and regrets not cutting AMC too. "Your coins can always go lower." A thesis of "it has to bottom because it is low" is not a thesis.
7. **Clear the scope.** Clear migrated junk with the exit-hide button regularly. Filters he now runs: global fee paid on migrated (because pump.fun boost mode made fake migrations profitable), volume filter, and must have socials linked. Strip data off token cards. Ten hours a day means every pixel costs attention.
8. **The real skill is knowing what is bad.** Most coins are bad. Time spent on sludge is how you miss runners back to back.
9. **Twitter tracker (J7).** Add the creator or claimed dev to the tracker immediately so you can get out if their wallet funds or dumps.
10. **Free trade pre-bond.** Sell initials before bond, then hold the rest for the home run. He did this on Pumpy Inu and bought more on dips.
11. **Low order count on FOMO is bullish.** When a coin has only ~23 FOMO orders with a good buy ratio, retail on phones is not in yet, and that helps price action.
12. **Buy the vamps while holding the main.** On a narrative runner, slam the copycats five sol each and flip them. It makes money, drives mindshare to the narrative, and kills the "so-and-so is in that one, let's vamp him" psychology. Never do this on tech or creator coins.
13. **Manage multiple positions.** His stated weakness. Beans, Cupsey and Gake hold 15 to 100 coins. There are 5 to 6 real runners at any time; being in all of them is the edge.
14. **Metas reflect demographics.** FOMO is onboarding normies, so narratives that translate to broad audiences and community coins (K-coin on TikTok) are back. Bear-market niche CT plays do not translate.
15. **Bearish on memecoin-to-memecoin pairs.** Beta holders want to flip the main, not support it (Brett vs Pepe, Shib vs Doge).
16. **After every dump, expect extractive tech or KOL meta.** Be careful what you size into.
17. **Longevity.** 10 hours a day, coffee and espresso, no energy drinks, know your limits. Discipline to sit focused for 10 hours beats 99% of participants.
18. **Why some new traders click in 3 weeks.** Intelligence, youth with TikTok intuition for virality, and gaming-trained ability to sit for hours.

Live example of his full read on OP_CAT, in order: simple buzzword narrative ("cat quantum-proofs Bitcoin"), current-events tie-in (quantum and Bitcoin tweets), real creator with soft endorsement and fee claim, not dependent on the creator tweeting, a cat meme for the FOMO normie, FOMO whales buying early, and it stood out from a scope full of coal. Everything else bonding was "zero in 20 minutes."

## The shared template (the overlaps)

These are the points both of them state independently, which is the template you asked for.

1. **Attention is the asset.** insentos: the only variable. Jack: understanding why something makes people tick is the whole skill.
2. **Comparison sets the ceiling.** insentos: SI's ceiling came from the earlier AI coin; "comparison is going to be your best friend." Jack: every coin is a derivative, know the original.
3. **Narrative over tech and celebrity.** insentos: no celebrity tokens. Jack: no dev-based or tech coins. Both: narrative coins have PvP but are the safest to size.
4. **Novelty.** insentos: it must be something never done. Jack: he buys what is "fresh, unique, pops out," and dismisses anything he has seen deployed 150 times.
5. **Write the catalysts before entry.** Both map what happens next and hold for that. AMC was the same trade for both and the same lesson for both.
6. **Who is buying.** insentos: FOMO leaderboard names. Jack: tracked wallets, FOMO whales early, J7.
7. **Sell when the catalyst lands.** insentos: sell the news at max virality. Jack: cut when you feel resistance, and his OP_CAT regret was not trimming into volume.
8. **It can always go lower.** Both round-tripped bags last month and both say the fix is a mental reset, smaller balance, back to fundamentals.
9. **Be online 8 to 10+ hours.** Both say the time on chain is the edge and the hardest part is staying focused.
10. **Virality intuition.** Both credit TikTok brain as the reason young traders click fast.

Written as a pre-trade checklist, the overlap reduces to six questions: Is it new? What past runner is it a derivative of and what did that top at? Who has bought it and who follows them? What are the next two catalysts? Which catalyst is the sell signal? Is it narrative rather than dev, tech, or celebrity dependent? If any answer is blank, it is a 2x trade at most, not a 100x candidate.

# Part 2. The 5 % that matters

The 5% is three physical facts, one daily loop, and one list of things to never do. Everything else in both videos is detail on top of these.

**The physics**

1. **Price is the flow of attention, not the level.** A coin rises while new eyes are arriving and falls the moment arrivals slow, even if everyone still likes it. AMC and SI both died while the story was still true. So you are never trading a coin, you are trading the second derivative of attention: is it accelerating or decelerating right now.
2. **Your only edge is recognition speed.** Everyone sees the same tape. Money goes to whoever classifies a new coin correctly fastest. That speed comes from one input: having seen the last thousand runners and their ceilings. This is why both of them say the same boring thing: hours on screen and studying trending with no trades on. There is no shortcut, which is why it is the edge.
3. **Daily money comes from the middle of the curve, not the ends.** insentos says it outright: the 100x template is a side pocket. Daily income is 2x and 3x on consensus runners, entered after the narrative is confirmed and exited before the catalyst lands. The 10k-to-1M end is gambling and the 100x end is rare. The middle is where the sizing works.

**The daily loop**

1. **Is there a live meta today?** If majors are down and the scope is tech, KOL, or launchpad coal, the answer is no and you do not trade. Two of the best traders alive went red in two hours because they traded a dead tape for a video. Not trading on a dead day is a trade.
2. **Classify before you look at the chart.** Narrative coin, or dev, tech, celebrity coin. Only the first is tradable. Jack's line: the skill is knowing what is bad, because that saves the attention you need for what is good.
3. **Name the comparable and the next catalyst.** Which past runner is this a derivative of and where did that top. What has to happen next for more eyes to arrive. If you cannot fill both blanks, size is a scalp or zero.
4. **Enter with the free-trade structure.** Take initials out at the first strong leg or pre-bond, then let the rest ride toward the catalyst. This converts every subsequent decision from fear to optionality.
5. **Exit on one of three triggers, decided before entry.** The catalyst lands (sell the news). Arrivals slow (volume and new holders flatten). You feel resistance in the broader meta (size everything down, immediately, even if wrong). "It can always go lower" replaces every "it has to bottom here."
6. **Study, then stop.** End with trending review: what ran, who bought, why. Ten focused hours, no stimulants, so you can repeat tomorrow.

**The never list**

- Never hold through the catalyst hoping for more. Both of their biggest regrets, AMC and OP_CAT, were this one error.
- Never trade dev-dependent, tech, or celebrity coins. Unbounded downside with no exit.
- Never chase vamps on a tech or creator coin. Only on a narrative runner you already hold.
- Never add money after a round trip. Reset with a smaller balance.

Everything else they said is tooling (J7, filters, scope hygiene), which is worth copying but is not where the money is. Prioritizing it over the loop above is how people spend ten hours a day and lose.
